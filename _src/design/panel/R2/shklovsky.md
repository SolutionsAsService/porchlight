# Critic: Provocateur (Shklovsky), R2

Anchor used: Shklovsky, "Art as Technique": habitualization devours objects; art makes the stone stony by impeding perception and prolonging it.

Observed: On the front page the left eye is now a full disc: a lit gibbous on the left, a dark brown rind on the right. Without its caption it reads as a moon, and the "Tonight's moon: 59% lit, waning gibbous" caption confirms it. The reader has to work out that a pumpkin eye is the moon, so perception is slowed usefully. The stencil caption now sits below the oval, and the oval no longer cuts through the words. On Stencil, Safety and About the band lantern is a small mark of about 90 px. Its lit feature differs by page: BOO on Stencil, the nose on Safety, the eye on About. Each page therefore re-uses the same object but lights a different part of it. The change is slight. After the first two pages the reader will probably treat it as the logo, not as a "you are here" signal. Fresh seeing depends on noticing that one part is lit. At that size the unlit features are nearly invisible (Safety), so the difference is easy to miss. I found no concrete defect.

Verdict: PASS

Requested change: none

Statement of meaning: A pumpkin whose eye is tonight's moon, and whose lit part tells you which room you are in.

Critic question: Can a reader who has not seen the front page name what is lit in the band, or does the lit part only register as decoration?
