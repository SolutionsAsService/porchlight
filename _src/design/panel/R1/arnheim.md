Critic: Formalist (Arnheim), R1

Anchor used: Arnheim, Art and Visual Perception: balance (visual weight, the centre, the structural skeleton), figure and ground, light.

Observed in the work:
- Balance: the pumpkin sits on the vertical axis (x~640), so the skeleton is a centred mass. The three left labels run y=190-590 as a stack of three heavy blocks; the two right labels (y=226-560) are spread wide with a gap between them. Weight is roughly matched, but not by sameness. Two blocks set at greater leverage from the centre balance three at the left, and the left column's right-aligned text hugs the pumpkin while the right column's left-aligned text also hugs it. This works. The left cluster is still heavier and denser than the right, and the left side is more crowded.
- Light: the carved features carry yellow-white gradients with dark rims, and they are the brightest and most saturated shapes on the screen. The BOO mouth, the eye and the candy read as lit from within, because the lightest value sits at each hole's centre and falls off toward the rim. The ground, though, is a cream page, not the night the brief names. The pumpkin skin is mid-bright orange, so the "glow" has little darkness to push against. Light needs a darker surround to read as emitted; here it reads as a pale fill and not as a flame.
- Inner pages: Dark o'clock and Candy math are plain cream pages with a left text column and an empty right half. Dark o'clock has one strong element (the dusk gradient bar), and the big numerals in rust red give some weight. None of the front page's figure/ground contrast or glow carries over; the lower two thirds of each page is blank.

Verdict: OBJECT. Concrete defect: the cream ground (shown in index-first-screen and dark-1280) denies the inner-glow reading, since there is no dark field for the light to emerge from, and the inner pages hold no figure at all.

Requested change: darken the ground behind the hero pumpkin (a night field, with the pumpkin as the one lit shape on it) and reuse that night field as a band on each inner page's title block.

Statement of meaning: a light is only a light against a dark.

Critic question: if the page were turned to night, would five labels still compete with the face for first look?
