# Critic: Provocateur (Shklovsky), R1

**Anchor used:** Viktor Shklovsky, "Art as Technique": habitualization devours objects; art exists to make the stone stony, to impede perception and prolong it.

**Observed in the work:**
- The first screen's pumpkin body is the most automatized Halloween image there is: a smooth orange vector ellipse with a green stem, ribs and a gradient. Seen alone, the eye skips it.
- The estrangement is in the carving, and it is real but partial. The left eye is a lit crescent-gibbous lozenge, a moon drawn from tonight's computed fraction (59% waning). The right eye is a wrapped sweet, the nose a warning triangle, and the mouth BOO in stencil. The face is read as inventory, and that slows the eye. A reader who sees a sweet where an eye should be stops for a beat.
- The moon eye is the one feature that is not an icon. It is a computed fact standing in for a stock triangle eye. Because it renders as a plain half-disc with no dark limb, it reads as a "D" until the caption ("Moon 59% lit, waning gibbous") explains it. The device works only with the label beside it.
- BOO in the mouth is the pumpkin's oldest word. The stencil typeface (bridged O and B counters) makes the letters look cut, which gives a small shock.
- The inner pages (Candy math, Stencil) use one template: a small yellow icon, an H1, a tan card. Nothing on them estranges anything. The one exception is the Stencil preview: black letters inside a dashed face oval, which shows how the carving is made.
- Defect on the Stencil page: the caption "Tape to the pumpkin, trace with a pin..." sits on the dashed oval and is struck through by it.

**Verdict:** PASS. The pumpkin is automatized, but the features are a working device and not decoration. The one concrete defect is a caption collision, which is outside my anchor.

**Requested change (one):** Draw the moon eye as the full disc, with the unlit part as dark uncut rind (earthshine-dark) beside the lit part. The eye is then visibly a moon in its phase, without the caption.

**Statement of meaning:** The pumpkin is a worn coin, and the carving turns it over so the reader sees the five tools as the face.

**One critic question:** If the captions were removed, would anyone know the left eye was tonight's moon rather than a "D"?
