Safety

9 short tips for tonight, each from the organisation that published it.

  1. 01

    Eat first, check later

    Give children a meal or snack before they go, and have them wait to eat any treats until you have looked them over at home.

    Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

  2. 02

    Wrapped treats only

    Keep only commercially wrapped treats. Throw away anything unwrapped, torn, discoloured or with tiny pinholes.

    Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration

  3. 03

    Be seen

    Choose bright costumes, add reflective tape or glow-in-the-dark details, and carry a flashlight after dark.

    Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

  4. 04

    Costumes that let them see and walk

    Prefer non-toxic face paint and hats to masks that block vision; keep costumes short enough not to trip on, and look for "flame resistant" on the label.

    Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

  5. 05

    Who goes with whom

    Always go with young children. Older children should go in a group, with a time to be back. No one goes into a home or a car for a treat.

    Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

  6. 06

    Crossing the street

    Stay on lit streets and sidewalks, cross together at crosswalks, and put phones and music away while walking and crossing.

    Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

  7. 07

    Food allergies

    Read labels; an allergic child should not eat home-baked treats, and should carry their prescribed emergency medicine.

    Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

  8. 08

    Teal pumpkins

    A teal pumpkin on a porch means that house has non-food treats, so children with food allergies can join in.

    Source: FARE

  9. 09

    The littlest ones

    For young children, take out choking hazards: gum, peanuts, hard sweets and small toys.

    Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration
